# Mumbai civic ideas: the full list

Forty-one ideas for the Claude Impact Lab Mumbai challenge: see where public money goes, track whether promises are kept, hold the right people and systems accountable.

Ideas 1 to 5 are planned in detail in [../mumbai-civic-graph/](../mumbai-civic-graph/). Ideas 6 to 41 are new and described in this folder. All of them can sit on the same foundation: the civic ontology and graph, the agent desks, and the tender portal pipeline already designed there.

## How to read the ratings

The ratings are my judgement, not measurements. Treat them as a starting point for discussion with people who know the system.

- **Value:** how much a Mumbai resident gains if it works. High means it changes what a person can do about a problem they already have.
- **Feasibility:** how likely a small team can ship a real first version. High means it needs only data that is already published and no one's permission.
- **Data:** *Open* (published today), *Partly* (some published, some gaps), *Ask* (needs RTI, a partner, or an agency's cooperation). Apart from the tender portals, I did not test these sources; confirm before committing.
- **First version:** *Days* (hackathon scale), *Weeks*, *Months*.

## Shortlists

**Buildable on the day, from published data, with clear value**

| Idea | Why |
|---|---|
| 14 Work Coming to Your Street | Uses only tender portal data already mapped; every resident understands it at once |
| 6 Unspent Money Watch | Needs only budget books; produces a striking, checkable number per department |
| 19 Safety List | Published lists, mapped; directly about people's safety |
| 32 Audit Para Tracker | Audit reports are public text; nobody tracks follow-up |
| 30 RTI Commons | No dependency on any agency; grows with use |
| 31 Complaint Closure Audit | Needs only residents' own evidence |

**Most likely to be adopted by the city**, because they help officers as well as citizens

| Idea | Why an officer wants it |
|---|---|
| 18 Pothole Ledger (with Jawabdar's liability watch) | Repairs at the contractor's cost, not the city's |
| 15 Site Board QR | Fewer enquiries and RTIs about ongoing works |
| 16 Monsoon Readiness | Early warning before the rains, not blame after |
| 39 Open Publishing Kit | Less manual reporting once data flows from source |

**High value, but need a partner or sustained effort**

23 Hospital Watch, 25 Water Ledger, 22 Reserved Plots Tracker, 28 Stalled Rehab Tracker, 36 Ward Participatory Budget. Do not attempt these on the day. Use them in the pitch as the direction of travel.

## A suggested combination

If the team wants one product rather than a list, these six fit together and reuse each other's data:

1. **Work Coming to Your Street** (14) brings residents in with something immediately useful.
2. **Paisa Trail** (1) and **Jawabdar** (2) answer "what did it cost and who is responsible".
3. **Pothole Ledger** (18) and **Complaint Closure Audit** (31) turn residents' reports into evidence.
4. **RTI Commons** (30) fills the gaps the published data leaves.

All six read from and write to the same graph.

## Rules that apply to every idea

These come from the detailed plans and are not repeated in each entry.

- Every fact shown carries its source document, page and quote. No source, no claim.
- Show records, not verdicts. Patterns are "worth checking", never proof of wrongdoing.
- Officials appear by post. Elected representatives appear in their public role only.
- Residents' photos have faces and number plates blurred before storage.
- Nothing about a named party is published without a person reviewing it first.
- Do not defeat access controls such as captchas. Ask for data, or collect it by hand at a polite rate.
