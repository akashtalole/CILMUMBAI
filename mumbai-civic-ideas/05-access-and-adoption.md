# Access and adoption

Ideas 37 to 40. These are not separate products. They decide whether any of the other ideas reaches the people who need it and survives after the event.

---

## 37. Voice Line

**Problem.** Many of the residents with most at stake do not read English, are not comfortable with apps, or share a phone. A website does not reach them.

**What residents get.** A phone number and a WhatsApp number. Speak in Marathi or Hindi: "Who is responsible for the broken drain in our lane?" Hear the answer, and receive the drafted complaint as a message that can be forwarded or printed at a nearby shop.

**How.** Speech-to-text in front of the citizen desk agent and text-to-speech behind it. The agent already replies in the resident's language and asks for a location when it lacks one. Speech services are separate products to choose and test for Marathi quality.

**First version.** Weeks. WhatsApp voice notes first; a call-in line later.

**Watch out.** Test with real speakers from the areas served; accents and place names are where transcription fails. Always read back the location heard before answering.

---

## 38. Newsroom Feed

**Problem.** Local reporters, especially in Marathi papers, cover civic affairs with little time and no data desk. A finding that is never reported changes nothing.

**What reporters get.** A weekly brief: the largest awards, works that passed their deadline, allocations with nothing tendered, audit findings still unanswered. Each item has its documents attached and a note of what is not known.

**How.** The audit desk's ward brief, across all wards, with the Challenger's review included so reporters see the counter-argument up front.

**First version.** Days, once any audit output exists.

**Watch out.** Give it to all outlets on the same terms. Mark clearly what is official record and what is residents' report.

---

## 39. Open Publishing Kit

**Problem.** Everything here depends on reading PDFs after the fact. That is fragile and always late. The lasting fix is for the city to publish tenders, awards, works and payments as data.

**What the city gets.** A small, documented format based on the open contracting standard and the civic ontology; a validator; and a converter from the fields its systems already hold. Departments report once, at source, and stop answering the same questions by letter.

**How.** The ontology and validation shapes already written for the graph become the specification. A pilot with one department and one ward demonstrates it.

**First version.** Months, and only with the city's IT department as a partner.

**Watch out.** This is the adoption conversation, not a build. Lead with what it saves the city, and offer the tools built at the event as proof that the data is used.

---

## 40. Public Data Service

**Problem.** One team cannot build every tool the city needs. Others, including students, newsrooms and residents' associations, could build their own if the data were reachable.

**What builders get.** The civic graph as an open, read-only service: a documented API, bulk downloads under an open licence, and a tool interface that AI assistants can query directly with sources returned for every fact.

**How.** The read-only graph tools already defined for the agents, hosted behind rate limits. The same service backs the scheduled, unattended agent runs described in the multi-agent plan.

**First version.** Weeks after the graph is stable.

**Watch out.** Publish only official-tier and reviewed data in bulk. Keep residents' reports and anything under review out of downloads.
